🧬 Syncytial Knot Isolation Device

Microfluidic Sorting Solutions for Placental Pathology

Developing microfluidic diagnostics to isolate syncytial knots ($50\text{–}150\text{ }\mu\text{m}$) from placenta tissue architectures. This structural research aids the clinical identification of early preeclampsia and fetal growth restriction constraints.

The Separation Challenge

Shed trophoblast units map early clinical markers of pregnancy complications. However, conventional manual picking, centrifugation, and standard membrane lines struggle with significant structural limits:

  1. Manual picking protocols induce high labor burdens and operator variances.
  2. Centrifugation shear levels easily fracture sensitive multinucleated tissue structures.
  3. Basic dead-end membrane filter setups foul quickly due to dense background cellular debris layers.

Design Architectures Explored

Our team researched, modeled, and structured three microfluidic separation systems to optimize continuous, high-yield particle extraction.

1. Deterministic Lateral Displacement (DLD)

Array Sorting Strategy

⛯ Microfluidic Mechanism

Employs an ordered, asymmetric shifting pillar array grid. Particles larger than the computed critical hydrodynamic cutoff radius ($D_c$) are displaced laterally along the array vectors, while smaller background items pass straight through.

⟳ Design Iterations

  • V1: Standard uniform cylindrical pillar rows; high pressure drops noted.
  • V2: Shifted to optimized airfoil-shaped pillars to reduce localized shear gradients.

⚠️ Technical Challenges

High susceptibility to macro-clogging at input boundaries. The tight, micro-patterned gaps can trap large cell debris clusters, shifting flow streamlines and changing downstream cutoff boundaries.

2. Crossflow Filtration

Shear-Driven Separation

⛯ Microfluidic Mechanism

Drives sample flow parallel to a slotted micro-barrier wall. Small matrix debris lines cross through the barrier elements via perpendicular pressure steps, while large target knots stay in the primary stream channel.

⟳ Design Iterations

  • V1: Straight side channels; observed high particle loss near processing boundaries.
  • V2: Angled layout structures with a $35\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ pillar offset window to improve flow profile distributions.

⚠️ Technical Challenges

Maintaining an ideal, even trans-barrier pressure balance. Slight variations in fluidic resistance fields can reverse local flow steps, letting target samples slip into the waste lines.

3. Spiral Inertial Microfluidics

Hydrodynamic Focused Sorting

⛯ Microfluidic Mechanism

Utilizes curved channel hydrodynamics to establish Dean vortices. The balance between inertial lift forces ($F_L$) and Dean drag forces ($F_D$) focuses target cell lines into crisp, distinct equilibrium channels based on size.

⟳ Design Iterations

  • V1: Uniform symmetric rectangular spiral setup; yielded loose separation zones.
  • V2: Shifted to a trapezoidal cross-section model to compress and sharpen velocity distributions.

⚠️ Technical Challenges

Requires high, tightly regulated operational flow rates. High input pressure lines increase the risk of PDMS channel deformation or bonding failures during bench validation testing.
