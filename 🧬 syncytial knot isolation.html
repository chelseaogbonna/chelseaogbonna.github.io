🧬 Syncytial Knot Isolation Device

Microfluidic Sorting Solutions for Placental Pathology

Designed and developed a microfluidic device to isolate syncytial knots from placental explant tissue. To improve research into maternal and fetal health, this project combines biomedical engineering, CAD, COMSOL simulations, microfabrication, and experimental validation.

Explore Design Approaches ↓

🩺 Clinical Background

Syncytial knots are multinucleated trophoblast structures naturally shed from the placenta throughout pregnancy. Their concentration and morphology are direct biomarkers associated with numerous high-risk pregnancy complications, including preeclampsia, fetal growth restriction (FGR), hypoxia, and placental dysfunction.

Historically, extracting these knots from biological tissue has been incredibly difficult. This is because there are other contaminants found in the placental tissue ie exosomes, single cell debris, ECM aggregates. Conventional centrifugation methods introduce physical stress that ruptures cellular envelopes, while membrane filters clog quickly due to dense background debris.

🔬 Importance of This Project

Developing a reliable, high-yield isolation platform creates groundbreaking diagnostic and therapeutic pathways:

  1. Early Detection: Spotting changes in syncytial knot count allows clinicians to identify placental failure long before symptoms appear.
  2. Prenatal Genetics: Isolating intact knots provides high-quality fetal DNA and intact nuclei, drastically improving non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT) resolution.
  3. Targeted Drug Delivery: Since syncytial knots circulate the maternal body without triggers, understanding their surface mechanics opens up safe, rejection-free drug carriage research.

Biological

  • Increase the rate of obtaining knots
  • Decrease manual labor (<25% manual labor)

Design

  • Microfluidic must be with 200 µm for height
  • Minimal clogging
  • Low pressure drop

Experimental Setup

Design Architectures Explored

Our team researched, modeled, and structured three microfluidic separation systems. These cards host high-impact visuals capturing our mechanism layouts, video test sweeps, and redesign iterations.

1. Deterministic Lateral Displacement (DLD)

Pillar Array Trajectory

⛯ Overview & Mechanism

Uses an offset, asymmetric micropillar array to separate particles by size, with particles larger than the critical diameter (D_c) displaced laterally while smaller particles continue straight along the fluid streamlines.

▶ Design In Action

High-resolution microscopic feed demonstrating bead trajectories and sorting boundaries inside the main displacement channel under constant flow rate.

⚠️ Encountered Challenges

Air bubbles disrupted flow through the pillar array, reducing separation reliability. Particles occasionally entered the incorrect outlet due to outlet flow distribution.

⟳ Proposed Redesign

Integrate a bubble trap or serpentine inlet and flush the device with increased pressure to remove trapped bubbles. Redesigned the outlet by reducing the number of pillars and adding a physical divider to improve stream separation.

2. Spiral Inertial Microfluidics

Inertial Sorting Velocity

⛯ Overview & Mechanism

Uses curved spiral channels to separate particles by size through the combined effects of inertial lift forces and Dean flow.

▶ Design In Action

Steady-state visualization of distinct particle streams aligning along separate cross-sectional coordinates before split-outlet harvesting.

⚠️ Encountered Challenges

Air bubbles disrupted inertial and Dean flow, causing particles to follow incorrect trajectories.

⟳ Proposed Redesign

Improve syringe loading, manually flush trapped bubbles, and incorporate bubble trap.

3. Crossflow Filtration

Slotted Micro-Barrier

⛯ Overview & Mechanism

The sample flows tangentially along the main channel, where a size-selective pillar array directs smaller particles into the permeate outlet while larger particles remain in the primary flow and continue downstream to the retentate outlet.

▶ Design In Action

Real-time verification showing parallel sweep mechanics and small background particle clearance without dead-end filter blockages.

⚠️ Encountered Challenges

Leakage between adjacent channels reduced separation efficiency. Leakage also occurred near inlet and outlet connections under higher pressures.

⟳ Proposed Redesign

Increase spacing between channels and redesign the device layout to improve structural isolation. Reinforce inlet/outlet interfaces and redesign connection geometry to improve sealing and pressure tolerance.

Challenges & Solutions

Throughout development, several engineering challenges affected device performance. Each issue required iterative testing and design modifications to improve reliability and repeatability.

⚠️

Particle Settling During Syringe Preparation

Challenge

The 100 μm polystyrene beads settled rapidly due to their relatively large size and density. This caused inconsistent particle concentrations between experiments and reduced repeatability.

Solution

The suspension was vortexed for 5–10 seconds immediately before each experiment and gently mixed prior to loading. Syringes were filled directly through the needle, and experiments were started immediately after preparation to minimize settling.

🧪

Air Bubbles in the Microfluidic Device

Challenge

Air bubbles frequently disrupted flow through the device, altered particle trajectories, and occasionally blocked sections of the microchannels, reducing separation efficiency.

Solution

Syringes were carefully filled and degassed before testing. The system was flushed prior to introducing the sample, and future designs could incorporate an upstream bubble trap to prevent bubbles from entering the device.

Challenge

Solution

📊

Particle Settling Inside the Syringe Pump

Challenge

During longer experiments, particles settled toward the back of the syringe while mounted on the syringe pump. This produced an uneven particle concentration throughout testing.

Solution

A mechanical shaker was first tested to keep particles suspended, but it did not produce enough vertical motion to effectively resuspend the beads. The syringe pump was instead positioned in a near-vertical orientation, which reduced bead accumulation and improved the consistency of particle delivery throughout testing.

⚠️ System-Wide Considerations

Beyond the three fixes above, scaling the platform raised broader trade-offs across all three device architectures:

  • Throughput vs. Cell Integrity: Scaling separation throughput without subjecting delicate multinucleated syncytial knots to damaging shear gradients.
  • Consistent Delivery: Maintaining a uniform particle concentration across an entire run, not just at the moment of loading.

🚀 Future Work

Building on the solutions above, next steps for the platform include:

  • Automated Agitation: Add a syringe mixer or continuous agitation system to maintain a homogeneous suspension without manual intervention.
  • Upstream Bubble Trap: Integrate a dedicated bubble trap into future device designs to prevent air from entering the channel network at all.
  • Clinical Validation: Transition from fluorescent control microparticles to processing human placenta explant specimens.

Experimental Validation

To evaluate device performance, multiple experiments were designed to assess particle separation efficiency, flow stability, cytotoxicity, and biological identification of isolated syncytial knots.

LDH Cytotoxicity Assay

An LDH assay will evaluate whether the microfluidic device preserves cell viability by measuring membrane damage after processing.

Planned Experiment

Immunofluorescence

DAPI and Cytokeratin-7 staining will confirm the biological identity of isolated syncytial knots following device separation. Then will be used to quantify particle recovery and verify successful size-based separation.
