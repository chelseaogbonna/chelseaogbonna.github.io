Inspire Labs

Bridging the STEM Achievement Gap Through Accessible, Community-Driven Learning

01 Project Overview & Problem Landscape

The Historical Problem

Project Excite was a collaborative effort between Northwestern University and Evanston Township High School to close the academic achievement and opportunity gap for minority and low-income students. This works by providing after-school and summer STEM classes for underrepresented students. They successfully increased participation of students of color in advanced STEM coursework however this initiatives required significant financial support and eventually became unsustainable. Students continued to face barriers including limited access to engaging STEM enrichment, a lack of relatable STEM role models, and reduced confidence in pursuing advanced academic opportunities.

Ecosystem Definition

This is where our model comes in. Inspire Labs is a community-centered STEM education initiative designed to reduce the achievement gap among elementary school students of color by making science education more accessible, engaging, and culturally relevant. The program combines hands-on experiment kits with short YouTube tutorials created by high school student mentors, allowing students to complete curriculum-aligned STEM activities from home while seeing role models who share similar backgrounds.

Rather than creating another after-school program, our team focused on designing a scalable, low-cost educational ecosystem that could continue supporting students even outside the classroom. The solution leveraged existing school resources, student volunteers, and online content to maximize accessibility while minimizing operational costs.

Primary Outreach Interface

Our Core Challenge

Our challenge was to design an educational program that could successfully:

  • Increase STEM engagement
  • Build self-confidence
  • Provide representation through mentorship
  • Align with elementary school curriculum
  • Be significantly less expensive than existing programs
  • Scale across multiple schools

02 User Research & Discovery Insights

Before designing the solution, we conducted interviews, classroom observations, and user testing with educators to better understand the needs of students, teachers, and families.

Client Interview

We interviewed our project sponsor to understand why the achievement gap persisted despite equal access to school resources.

Key Findings:
  • Lack of belonging often outweighed lack of ability.
  • Many students did not see successful STEM professionals who looked like them.
  • Previous programs succeeded because they made learning enjoyable rather than simply more rigorous.

Classroom Observation

Our team observed an elementary STEM classroom to understand how students naturally interacted with educational content.

Key Findings:
  • Students were highly engaged during game-based and hands-on activities.
  • Technology was widely available through school-issued Chromebooks.
  • Students preferred interactive learning over passive educational software.
  • Teachers emphasized the importance of maintaining curiosity through experimentation.

Expert User Testing

We presented our concept to education specialists, classroom teachers, and intervention specialists.

Key Improvements Highlighted:
  • Increase parent support
  • Keep videos short and easy to understand
  • Align experiments directly with classroom curriculum
  • Develop a sustainable funding model
  • Include teachers throughout implementation
  • Ensure accessibility for families with diverse backgrounds

Derived Design Requirements

From our research synthesis loops, we established several key design requirements:

  • Accessible regardless of household income
  • Fun and engaging for elementary students
  • Videos under five minutes
  • Curriculum aligned
  • Easy for families to follow independently
  • Culturally relevant
  • Measurable student outcomes
  • Representation through relatable mentors

03 Integrated Platform Architecture

Inspire Labs evolved into an integrated educational platform consisting of four connected components.

📦

1. Experiment Kits

Monthly boxes containing all supplies, instructions, and navigation QR loops.

🎥

2. YouTube Platform

Short tutorials featuring high school mentors sharing personal stories.

🤝

3. Mentor Program

Student-led ETHS recruitment pipeline for content generation loops.

💻

4. Parent Community

Online support hub for tracking experiment progress and active troubleshooting.

STEM Experiment Kits Detailed Framework

Students receive monthly experiment boxes containing all required materials, printed instructions, and QR codes linking directly to instructional videos. The kits eliminate the need for families to purchase additional supplies while making STEM activities accessible from home.

YouTube Learning Platform Detailed Framework

Each experiment is accompanied by a short instructional video hosted by high school students of color. Beyond demonstrating the experiment, many videos include personal stories and introductions that help younger students develop meaningful connections with relatable role models.

High School Mentor Program Structure

A student-led Inspire Labs club recruits volunteers from Evanston Township High School to develop educational content, record videos, and mentor younger students. This creates a sustainable mentorship pipeline while providing leadership opportunities for high school students.

Parent Support Community Infrastructure

Families are connected through an online parent community where they can ask questions, troubleshoot experiments, share progress, and receive ongoing support throughout the program.

Design Highlights

Representation Matters: Instead of using professional presenters, we intentionally selected high school students of color as instructors. This design decision provided elementary students with mentors they could identify with while promoting confidence and belonging in STEM fields.

Curriculum Alignment: Every experiment was designed around Illinois third-grade science standards so that activities reinforced concepts students were already learning in school instead of adding unrelated enrichment.

Accessibility: The platform minimized barriers by delivering materials directly, using YouTube for universal access, embedding QR codes for simple navigation, keeping videos short/age-appropriate, and supporting parents via an online community framework.

04 Project Impact & Future Scaling Matrix

Targeted Systemic Intentions

By combining hands-on experimentation, mentorship, and accessible digital learning, Inspire Labs addressed multiple factors contributing to educational inequity. The proposed program aimed to:

  • Increase STEM engagement among elementary students
  • Improve academic confidence
  • Strengthen representation in STEM education
  • Create sustainable mentorship opportunities
  • Provide a scalable, lower-cost alternative to previous outreach programs
  • Foster stronger partnerships between elementary schools, high schools, and families

Future Development Pathways

Future iterations of Inspire Labs could further expand its impact by:

  • Translating materials into multiple languages
  • Hosting in-person STEM workshops and science fairs
  • Creating an alumni mentorship network
  • Expanding into additional school districts
  • Partnering with corporate sponsors and nonprofit organizations to improve long-term sustainability

What I Learned

This project strengthened my ability to apply engineering design principles to complex social challenges. I learned how qualitative research, stakeholder interviews, and iterative user feedback can guide the development of solutions that extend beyond technical functionality to create meaningful community impact. Working on Inspire Labs reinforced the importance of designing systems that are not only innovative, but also accessible, scalable, and centered around the needs of the people they serve.
