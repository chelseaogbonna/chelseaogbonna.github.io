01 Project Overview & Problem Framework

Ecosystem Definition: Inspire Labs is a community-centered STEM education initiative designed to reduce the achievement gap among elementary school students of color by making science education more accessible, engaging, and culturally relevant. The program combines hands-on experiment kits with short YouTube tutorials created by high school student mentors, allowing students to complete curriculum-aligned STEM activities from home while seeing role models who share similar backgrounds.

Rather than creating another localized after-school program, our team focused on designing a scalable, low-cost educational ecosystem that could continue supporting students outside the classroom. The solution intentionally leveraged existing school resources, student volunteers, and online distribution content to maximize reach while minimizing structural operational overhead.

The Problem Space: Although historic structural programs such as Project Excite successfully increased participation of students of color in advanced STEM coursework, these initiatives required significant financial support and eventually became unsustainable. Students continued to face foundational barriers including limited access to engaging STEM enrichment, a lack of relatable STEM role models, and reduced confidence in pursuing advanced academic tracks.

02 User Research & Discovery Insights

To properly map user behaviors and constraints before structuring the engineering parameters, we deployed a rigorous three-tiered triangulation research protocol involving client deep-dives, classroom observational audits, and expert prototype test loops.

Client Stakeholder Interviews

Interviewed our project sponsor to unpack why the localized achievement gap persisted despite equal access to typical school resources.

Key Findings:
  • A lack of institutional belonging often outweighed a lack of technical ability.
  • Many students could not name successful STEM professionals who shared their backgrounds.
  • Previous platforms succeeded when prioritizing enjoyment over standard, dry rigor.

Classroom Observational Audits

Observed elementary school STEM classrooms to trace how students naturally interacted with technical content environments.

Key Findings:
  • Engagement metrics spiked during game-based and kinesthetic activities.
  • Hardware availability was ubiquitous via school-issued Chromebook infrastructure.
  • Students rejected passive software, favoring highly interactive media layouts.

Expert User Testing & Validation

Presented our structural outreach concepts directly to education specialists, classroom teachers, and specialized intervention teams.

Design Adjustments Made:
  • Integrated scaffolding loops to improve parent-at-home support.
  • Shortened video production criteria for easy comprehension.
  • Mapped experiments cleanly to specific classroom state curriculum milestones.

System Highlights & Design Optimization:

Representation Engineering: Instead of employing adult professional presenters, we intentionally recruited high school students of color from Evanston Township High School as content creators. This design pivot gives elementary students immediate mentors they can easily identify with, rapidly lowering psychological barriers while establishing confidence within technical domains.

State Standard Alignment: Every interactive module was reverse-engineered to fit the official Illinois third-grade science standards. This ensured that home activities directly reinforced active classroom lessons rather than introducing disconnected, distracting cognitive strain.

Engineering Design Reflection: This project strengthened my ability to apply rigorous engineering design principles to complex social challenges. Working on Inspire Labs reinforced the importance of designing systems that are not only innovative, but also accessible, scalable, and centered around the needs of the people they serve.
