Inspire Labs

Bridging the STEM Achievement Gap Through Accessible, Community-Driven Learning
Timeline Bounds

April 2023 – June 2023

Assigned Engineering Role

Human-Centered Design Engineer | Researcher | Curriculum & Program Designer

Applied Core Skillsets
Human-Centered Design User Research Educational Design Stakeholder Interviews Prototype Development Curriculum Design Video Production Planning Community Outreach Systems Thinking

Project Overview

Inspire Labs is a community-centered STEM education initiative designed to reduce the achievement gap among elementary school students of color by making science education more accessible, engaging, and culturally relevant. The program combines hands-on experiment kits with short YouTube tutorials created by high school student mentors, allowing students to complete curriculum-aligned STEM activities from home while seeing role models who share similar backgrounds.

Rather than creating another after-school program, our team focused on designing a scalable, low-cost educational ecosystem that could continue supporting students even outside the classroom. The solution leveraged existing school resources, student volunteers, and online content to maximize accessibility while minimizing operational costs.

The Problem & The Challenge

The Context

Although historical initiatives such as Project Excite successfully increased participation of students of color in advanced STEM coursework, these initiatives required significant financial support and eventually became unsustainable over time.

The Persistent Barriers

When these heavily funded programs ended, students continued to face systemic barriers including limited access to engaging STEM enrichment outside school hours, a lack of relatable STEM role models, and reduced confidence in pursuing advanced academic paths.

The Core Design Challenge

Our explicit engineering challenge was to design an integrated educational program capable of executing against six interdependent targets:

  • Increase baseline STEM engagement metrics inside target student groups.
  • Build resilient academic self-confidence.
  • Provide localized representation through programmatic mentorship pipelines.
  • Align experiments explicitly with elementary school classroom curricula.
  • Operate significantly less expensively than historic outreach paradigms.
  • Scale cleanly across multiple variable school systems.

User Research & Discovery

Before formulating technical design parameters, we conducted targeted stakeholder interviews, field-level classroom observations, and active user testing loops with educators to map the explicit needs of students, teachers, and families.

Stakeholder Phase 1: Client Interview

We interviewed our project sponsor to analyze why the achievement gap persisted despite equal access to school-day resources.

Key Sponsor Discoveries

  • A lack of systemic belonging often outweighed a lack of technical ability.
  • Many students did not see successful STEM professionals who looked like them.
  • Previous programs succeeded because they made learning enjoyable rather than simply more rigorous.

Stakeholder Phase 2: Classroom Observation

Our team observed an active elementary STEM classroom environment to track how students naturally interacted with educational content.

Key Field Observations

  • Students exhibited peak engagement indicators during game-based and hands-on structural activities.
  • Hardware technology access was widely available via school-issued Chromebook infrastructures.
  • Students preferred interactive physical learning configurations over passive, closed educational software.
  • Teachers emphasized the overarching importance of maintaining core curiosity through open experimentation.

Stakeholder Phase 3: Expert User Testing

We presented our early concept models directly to education specialists, classroom teachers, and dedicated intervention specialists.

Formative Feedback Adjustments

  • Increase explicit at-home parent support materials.
  • Keep all digital video modules short and highly direct for easy comprehension.
  • Align home experiments directly with classroom-taught curriculum nodes.
  • Develop a highly sustainable, low-cost funding model.
  • Include classroom teachers directly throughout all stages of localized implementation.
  • Ensure complete platform accessibility for families with diverse backgrounds.

The Final Integrated Solution

Inspire Labs evolved past isolated components into an integrated educational platform consisting of four connected operational pillars:

🧪 STEM Experiment Kits

Students receive monthly experiment boxes containing all required physical materials, printed instructional guides, and integrated QR codes linking directly to designated instructional videos. The kits fully eliminate the need for families to purchase additional baseline supplies while making STEM activities uniformly accessible from home.

🎥 YouTube Learning Platform

Each experiment is accompanied by a short instructional video asset hosted entirely by high school students of color. Beyond demonstrating the basic mechanical experiment steps, many videos intentionally include personal stories and introductions that help younger students develop meaningful psychological connections with relatable role models.

🤝 High School Mentor Program

A student-led Inspire Labs club recruits active volunteers from Evanston Township High School to develop educational content, record videos, and mentor younger students. This structures a sustainable, cascading mentorship pipeline while providing leadership development loops for high school students.

💻 Parent Support Community

Families are seamlessly connected through an online parent community portal where they can ask questions, troubleshoot experiment failure modes, share student progress, and receive ongoing platform support throughout the program.

Design Highlights

Representation Matters

Instead of opting for professional adult presenters, we intentionally selected high school students of color as primary instructors. This design decision directly provided elementary students with accessible mentors they could naturally identify with while actively promoting confidence and long-term belonging in STEM fields.

Curriculum Alignment

Every physical experiment was reverse-engineered around Illinois third-grade science standards so that all at-home activities cleanly reinforced concepts students were already learning in school, avoiding unrelated or distracting enrichment strain.

Accessibility Optimization

The platform systematically minimized historic participation barriers by:

  • Delivering all required experiment materials directly to students' homes.
  • Utilizing YouTube as a universal, free-access digital distribution ecosystem.
  • Including QR codes for simplified navigation across household devices.
  • Keeping instructional videos short, high-energy, and age-appropriate.
  • Supporting parents through an active, low-friction online community framework.

Project Impact & Future Trajectories

Anticipated Systemic Impact

By combining hands-on experimentation, peer mentorship, and accessible digital learning architectures, Inspire Labs addresses multiple systemic factors contributing to educational inequity:

  • Increase measurable STEM engagement indicators among elementary cohorts.
  • Improve foundational academic self-confidence.
  • Strengthen representation structures inside local STEM education.
  • Create highly sustainable, self-generating student mentorship loops.
  • Provide a highly scalable, significantly lower-cost alternative to previous outreach investments.
  • Foster stronger communication partnerships between elementary schools, high schools, and families.

Future Development Trajectories

Future structural iterations of Inspire Labs are configured to expand impact via:

  • Translating all print and media materials into multiple heritage languages.
  • Hosting local, in-person STEM workshops and integrated community science fairs.
  • Creating an interconnected alumni mentorship network to track long-term student trajectories.
  • Expanding the distribution framework into additional school districts.
  • Partnering with corporate sponsors and nonprofit networks to reinforce long-term structural sustainability.

System Requirements Matrix

Rigorous design requirements derived directly from discovery phase triangulation:

Economic Bound

Fully accessible regardless of volatile household income brackets; zero out-of-pocket kit expenses.

Engagement Standard

Activities must be highly interactive and fun to keep elementary students intrinsically motivated.

Temporal Constraint

All digital instructional video modules must remain strictly under five minutes to match target cognitive attention spans.

Curriculum Target

Directly mapped to Illinois third-grade science standards to provide classroom lesson scaffolding.

User Autonomy

Kits must be completely safe, intuitive, and simple enough for families to deploy independently without professional oversight.

Cultural Relevance

Content must represent diverse student backgrounds to build immediate psychological safety and inclusion.

Measurability

Program framework must include feedback mechanisms to assess student growth and participation.

What I Learned
