Wearable Diabetic Foot Monitoring System

Embedded Sensor Integration & Kinematic Gait Analysis

Problem Statement

Diabetic foot ulcers are among the most common and severe complications of diabetes, often leading to infection, hospitalization, and lower-limb amputation. Many ulcers develop gradually due to repetitive pressure and abnormal gait patterns that patients cannot detect because of peripheral neuropathy.

Traditional clinical assessments provide only periodic snapshots of a patient's walking behavior and miss early warning signs of tissue damage. The goal of this project was to develop a wearable sensing system capable of continuously monitoring gait characteristics to identify movement patterns associated with increased ulcer risk.

The Experiment

To track kinematic and kinetic parameters continuously, a custom in-shoe hardware prototype was designed and evaluated under controlled walking trials:

  • Sensing Architecture: Positioned Force Sensitive Resistors (FSRs) beneath high-risk plantar loading zones to measure dynamic force distribution, paired with an MPU6050 Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) to capture 3-axis acceleration and angular velocity.
  • Data Acquisition: Integrated a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 to stream real-time sensor payloads over custom low-latency Python software loops.
  • Execution Strategy: Conducted successive walking trials along uniform baselines to collect synchronized datasets, minimizing variability and validating hardware timing accuracy across continuous intervals.

Results

The system successfully cross-referenced sensor streams to map individual strides cleanly:

  • Kinetic Capture: Force profiles successfully targeted changes in localized plantar load distribution across the complete stance phases.
  • Kinematic Capture: IMU spatial measurements accurately isolated swing and strike acceleration signatures, defining distinct cadence metrics.
  • Sensor Fusion: Combining force map inputs directly alongside inertial patterns provided a comprehensive diagnostic view of gait stability that single-modality tracking systems miss.

Discussion

This work confirms that pairing in-sole pressure maps with foot-motion telemetry provides a reliable framework for remote, real-time biomechanical monitoring. While future clinical deployments will require diagnostic validation with neuropathic patient paths, this system offers a functional foundation for early ulcer screening devices.

From an engineering perspective, developing this platform directly strengthened core skills across hardware-software co-design, real-time sensor synchronization, digital signal filtering, and experimental research execution.
