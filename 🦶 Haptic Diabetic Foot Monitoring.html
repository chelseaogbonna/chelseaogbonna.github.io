🧦 Wearable Diabetic Foot Monitoring System

Multimodal In-Shoe Kinematic & Kinetic Gait Analysis

Developed an integrated wearable hardware platform utilizing synchronized plantar pressure mapping and inertial tracking array systems. This system continuously monitors quantitative gait parameters to capture diagnostic insights into high-risk patterns linked to diabetic neuropathy and foot ulcer development.

Problem Statement

Diabetic foot ulcers are among the most common and severe complications of diabetes, often leading to infection, hospitalization, and lower-limb amputation. Many ulcers develop gradually due to repetitive pressure and abnormal gait patterns that patients cannot detect because of peripheral neuropathy.

Traditional clinical assessments provide only periodic snapshots of a patient's walking behavior and may miss early warning signs of tissue damage. The goal of this project was to develop a wearable sensing system capable of continuously monitoring gait characteristics and providing data that could be used to identify movement patterns associated with increased ulcer risk.

Walking Trials

Walking experiments were conducted to evaluate the wearable sensing system under controlled conditions while collecting synchronized force and motion data.

🏃‍♀️ Normal Walking

Participants walked at a comfortable pace across a predefined walking path to establish baseline gait characteristics.

🔁 Repeated Trials

Multiple walking trials were collected to ensure repeatability and reduce variability between individual recordings.

📊 Data Collection

Sensor measurements were recorded continuously throughout each walking trial and synchronized for later analysis.

Sensors Used

⚡ Force Sensitive Resistors (FSRs)

FSRs were positioned beneath the foot to measure plantar pressure throughout the gait cycle. Pressure distribution provides insight into areas experiencing repeated loading that may contribute to ulcer formation.

🧭 Inertial Measurement Unit (MPU6050)

The IMU measured three-axis acceleration and angular velocity to characterize foot motion, walking stability, cadence, and stride dynamics.

💻 Microcontroller

A Raspberry Pi Pico 2 collected sensor data in real time and transmitted the measurements for analysis using custom Python software.

Challenges Encountered

⚠️ Sensor Noise

Motion artifacts and electrical noise introduced variability into the sensor signals during walking.

📍 Sensor Placement

Small changes in sensor positioning influenced force measurements between trials.

⏱️ Data Synchronization

Maintaining synchronized force and IMU recordings required careful software timing and data management.

Engineering Solutions

📈 Signal Processing

Python filtering techniques reduced measurement noise while preserving important gait characteristics.

📐 Standardized Setup

Consistent sensor placement procedures improved repeatability across walking trials.

⚙️ Automated Analysis

Custom Python scripts synchronized sensor data and generated visualizations for rapid interpretation.

Results

Analysis demonstrated that combining force sensors with inertial measurement data provides a comprehensive representation of walking mechanics.

🦶 Pressure Data

Force sensors successfully identified changes in plantar loading throughout the gait cycle.

📉 Motion Data

IMU measurements captured acceleration patterns associated with walking and changes in foot movement.

🔀 Integrated Analysis

Combining both sensing modalities produced a more complete assessment of walking behavior than either sensor alone.

Discussion

This project demonstrated the value of integrating pressure sensing and inertial measurements for wearable gait analysis. While additional testing with diabetic patients is necessary, the platform provides a foundation for future systems capable of detecting gait abnormalities associated with diabetic foot ulcer development.

Beyond the clinical application, the project strengthened skills in embedded systems, sensor integration, signal processing, experimental design, and biomechanical data analysis.
