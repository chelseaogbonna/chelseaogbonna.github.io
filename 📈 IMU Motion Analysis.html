Low-Cost EKG Signal Acquisition System

Analog Signal Conditioning & Biosignal Processing

01 \ Problem Statement

Electrocardiograms (EKGs) are critical tools for monitoring cardiac rhythm and electrical activity, yet commercial monitoring systems remain costly and hard to access for low-resource or educational environments.

The core challenge stems from physics: cardiac biopotentials are microvolt-to-millivolt range signals. This leaves raw data highly vulnerable to interference from muscle contractions (EMG artifacts), respiration shifts, and environmental electromagnetic fields.

02 \ Design Approach & Setup

This system establishes clean biosignal acquisition utilizing basic, high-accessibility hardware components combined with focused multi-stage analog filter design blocks:

  • Filter Topology: Integrates a 1 Hz high-pass active filter block to arrest slow baseline wander alongside a tuned low-pass filter to block high-frequency muscle interference.
  • Ground Topology: Implements an intentional limb ground reference electrode placed on the elbow to reduce body-capacitance noise loops.

Engineering Challenges

High-amplitude power line artifacts (60 Hz noise) and sudden baseline wander from subject movement threatened to completely swamp the microscopic cardiac wave features.

Implemented Solutions

Careful analog filter component selection and tuning matched cutoffs precisely to specific physiological bandwidths while optimizing limb ground connection consistency to clear noise floor envelopes.

03 \ Results

Raw Hardware Biopotential Capture

The unconditioned signal input demonstrates the severity of environmental interference prior to entering the conditioning stages, masking critical diagnostic features.

Isolating Morphological Components

Post-filtering, the system successfully stabilizes the baseline drift, yielding a high-fidelity cardiac signature where the P wave, QRS complex, and T wave are sharply distinct.

Live System Demonstration

Real-time validation tracking demonstrating responsive hardware signal output stabilization and active trace mapping during human subject trials.

Summary of Findings: The final hardware loop proves that sophisticated, expensive instrumentation is not an absolute barrier to clear physiological data collection. With robust analog filtering and grounded designs, recognizable real-time waveforms are safely captured at a fraction of standard cost limits.

04 \ Discussion & Future Scope

The platform provides a modular foundation for remote diagnostic designs. Future revisions will focus on substituting discrete op-amps with precision instrumentation amplifiers, adding a dedicated 60 Hz notch filter stage, and transitioning to wearable wireless form factors.

Executing this development path strengthened core technical skills in low-noise analog layout design, biopotential interface fundamentals, filter validation, and electronic prototyping.
