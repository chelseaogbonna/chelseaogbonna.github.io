Low-Cost EKG Signal Acquisition System

Analog Signal Conditioning & Biosignal Processing

01 Problem Statement

Electrocardiograms (EKGs) are critical tools for monitoring cardiac rhythm and electrical activity, yet commercial monitoring systems remain costly and hard to access for low-resource or educational environments.

The core challenge stems from physics: cardiac biopotentials are microvolt-to-millivolt range signals. This leaves raw data highly vulnerable to interference from muscle contractions (EMG artifacts), respiration shifts, and environmental electromagnetic fields.

02 Design Approach & Setup

This system establishes clean biosignal acquisition utilizing basic, high-accessibility hardware components combined with focused multi-stage analog filter design blocks:

  • Filter Topology: Integrates a 1 Hz high-pass active filter block to arrest slow baseline wander alongside a tuned low-pass filter to block high-frequency muscle interference.
  • Ground Topology: Implements an intentional limb ground reference electrode placed on the elbow to reduce body-capacitance noise loops.

Engineering Challenges

One of the primary challenges was distinguishing the weak EKG signal from surrounding electrical noise. Small body movements, changes in electrode placement, and electromagnetic interference could significantly distort the waveform.

Implemented Solutions

Careful filter selection and grounding strategies were required to balance noise reduction while preserving critical cardiac features such as the P wave, QRS complex, and T wave.

03 Results

Live System Demonstration

Real-time validation tracking demonstrating responsive hardware signal output stabilization and active trace mapping during human subject trials.

Summary of Findings: The final circuit successfully captured recognizable EKG waveforms and demonstrated the effectiveness of analog filtering for biosignal acquisition. The filtering stages reduced baseline drift and high-frequency noise, producing a cleaner signal suitable for visualization and basic cardiac analysis.
