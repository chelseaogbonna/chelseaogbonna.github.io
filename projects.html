Syncytial Knot Isolation Microfluidic Device

Biomedical Research / Microfluidics

💡 Project Overview

Placental syncytial knots (SKs) are multicellular structures associated with pregnancy complications such as preeclampsia and fetal growth restriction. Current isolation techniques are labor-intensive, low-throughput, and rely heavily on manual processing. This project aimed to design a passive microfluidic platform capable of continuously separating syncytial knots from placental tissue based on size boundaries from $50\text{–}150\text{ }\mu\text{m}$.

🛠️ My Contributions

  • Led a multidisciplinary engineering team throughout the design process.
  • Designed, simulated, and fabricated DLD, crossflow, and spiral microfluidic devices using SolidWorks, COMSOL, and SUEX photoresist.
  • Developed experimental protocols and evaluated performance using fluorescent microparticles.

📈 Results

Successfully designed and tested multiple microfluidic prototypes while identifying the strengths and limitations of each separation method. Experimental testing informed several design iterations that improved particle sorting and reduced clogging.

🧠 Skills Utilized

COMSOL Multiphysics SolidWorks CAD Photolithography SUEX Photoresist Fluidic Assays

Engineering Challenges

Maintaining consistent particle flow was difficult due to particle settling, air bubbles, and channel leakage. Different microfluidic designs also introduced unique challenges, including outlet mixing and flow instability.

How They Were Solved

Optimized syringe preparation, redesigned outlet geometries, increased channel spacing, and proposed bubble traps and improved inlet designs to improve separation reliability.

Sustainable Recycling Center

Human-Centered Product Design

💡 Project Overview

A condominium recycling center experienced low recycling participation because residents were unsure what could be recycled and the collection area was difficult to navigate. This project redesigned the recycling center using human-centered design principles to improve accessibility, organization, and recycling behavior.

🛠️ My Contributions

  • Led the engineering design process from user research through implementation.
  • Designed custom recycling bins, cardboard collection systems, and educational signage using SolidWorks and Siemens NX.
  • Conducted user interviews and observations to identify barriers to proper recycling.

📈 Results

The redesigned recycling center increased recycling participation by approximately $40\%$ and received the Best Design Team Award. The final design improved user understanding while reducing contamination of recyclable materials.

🧠 Skills Utilized

Siemens NX SolidWorks Human-Centered Design User Research Rapid Prototyping

Engineering Challenges

Residents frequently placed non-recyclable materials in recycling bins because of confusing signage and poor system organization.

How They Were Solved

Developed intuitive collection stations, simplified instructional graphics, and redesigned the recycling layout to improve usability and reduce sorting errors.

Haptic Diabetic Foot Ulcer Detection

Wearable Medical Device

💡 Project Overview

Diabetic foot ulcers often develop because patients cannot detect excessive pressure or abnormal gait until significant tissue damage has already occurred. This project developed a wearable monitoring system capable of identifying gait abnormalities using motion and force sensors.

🛠️ My Contributions

  • Designed a wearable sensing system using an MPU6050 IMU and force-sensitive resistors.
  • Developed Python programs to analyze walking data and identify gait characteristics.
  • Integrated hardware and software to collect synchronized biomechanical data.

📈 Results

Successfully demonstrated the ability to collect and analyze gait information across different walking conditions. The system showed potential for non-invasive monitoring of patients at risk of diabetic foot ulcers.

🧠 Skills Utilized

Python MPU6050 IMU Force-Sensitive Resistors Signal Filtering Gait Kinematics

Engineering Challenges

Synchronizing force and motion data while minimizing sensor noise affected measurement accuracy.
